**Definitions given in the excerpts**

- "Emotional programming refers to the specific effects that the nature of the parents' interactions with a child during development have on the child's ability to separate emotionally and develop a self" [FSJ 9.2 Bowen Subjectivity, Homo Sapiens, and Science].
- "The phenomenon of emotional programming occurs through the transmission of information across generations in a family by relationships and learning, not genes" [Bowen Theory Secrets_Chapter09_Emotional_Programming].
- "The multigenerational transmission process includes the emotional programming of one generation by the generations preceding it" [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory].
- The central idea of the concept is "that the impact the emotional functioning of the members of one generation has on the emotional functioning of the members of the next generation is, in some areas, so precise and predictable that it represents a genetic-like transfer of information through relationships. In other words, information regulating individual development and behavior is encoded not only in the genes, but also in the relationship system" [FSJ 4.2 (3) Kerr, M. Bowen Theory and Evo Theory 119-175].

**How it is distinguished from behavioural transmission.** "Behavioral transmission and emotional programming both involve learning and relationships, but behavioral transmission usually refers to the content of the information transmitted across generations (such as a specific skill or belief) and emotional programming refers to a process between the generations" [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory].

**How it occurs**

- "Emotional programming occurs through the ongoing interactions parents and other family members have with a child during his developmental years. Some programming may occur during intrauterine development" [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory].
- "The programming results from the verbal, nonverbal, and behavioral interactions that occur within and between the generations. The most intense programming occurs in a person's parental triangle during development, but siblings and other family members have an important influence on it as well" [Bowen Theory Secrets_Chapter09_Emotional_Programming].
- "The more the parents focus on a child out of their own needs and fears, the more the child focuses automatically on the parents. This reciprocal and ongoing process, which can be harmonious or contentious, heightens the child's reactivity to parental attention, approval, expectations, and distress." Four "social cues" — parental attention, approval, expectations, and distress — are described as "consistent elements of the programming that occurs" [FSJ 9.2 Bowen Subjectivity, Homo Sapiens, and Science].
- "Differences in the intensity of family triangles result in siblings being programmed differently... Chronic anxiety drives the more intense focus, not the reality needs of the child." Parents "miss how they unwittingly program the child to need more of their energy and attention. Their unrealistic needs for and expectations of the child program him to have unrealistic needs for and expectations of them" [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory].

**What it affects.** "Emotional programming appears to affect a child's biochemical, physiological, psychological, and relationship functioning"; "The process is anchored in the emotional system but includes complex feeling and psychological components" [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory]. "People are not conscious of most of the emotional programming that occurs, but it affects myriad automatic behaviors that govern much of people's lives, for better and for worse. For example, the programming can intensify or deintensify fear reactions to social stimuli. Automatic responses have interconnected emotional, feeling, and subjective cognitive components" [Bowen Theory Secrets_Chapter09_Emotional_Programming]. Anxiety-driven parental over-involvement "not only heightens his or her reactivity to the cues, but it also constrains the child's ability to formulate thoughts, opinions, and ideas on their own" [FSJ 9.2 Bowen Subjectivity, Homo Sapiens, and Science].

**Link to differentiation and siblings.** "Emotional programming is an important process to consider for understanding the transmission of information across generations and it may also provide a link between role differentiation and level of differentiation" [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory]. The more focused-on child "develops more of a 'relationship orientation'"; the sibling growing up "freer of the anxiety-driven components of family emotional process" is "more self-contained, has a more separate and distinct 'self'"; "In other words, the siblings develop different levels of differentiation" [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory].
