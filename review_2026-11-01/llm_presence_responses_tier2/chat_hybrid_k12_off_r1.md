**Definition and mechanism**

Emotional programming is described as a phenomenon that "occurs through the transmission of information across generations in a family by relationships and learning, not genes" [Bowen Theory Secrets_Chapter09_Emotional_Programming]. It "results from the verbal, nonverbal, and behavioral interactions that occur within and between the generations," the "most intense programming occurs in a person's parental triangle during development, but siblings and other family members have an important influence on it as well," and "people are not conscious of most of the emotional programming that occurs" [Bowen Theory Secrets_Chapter09_Emotional_Programming].

A second source offers a narrower definition focused on separation and self: "Emotional programming refers to the specific effects that the nature of the parents' interactions with a child during development have on the child's ability to separate emotionally and develop a self" [FSJ 9.2 Bowen Subjectivity, Homo Sapiens, and Science].

**Relation to multigenerational transmission and behavioral transmission**

"Emotional programming is an important process to consider for understanding the transmission of information across generations and it may also provide a link between role differentiation and level of differentiation" [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory]. Bowen theory's "multigenerational transmission process is broader than what biologists generally mean by behavioral transmission. The multigenerational transmission process includes the emotional programming of one generation by the generations preceding it" [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory]. The two are distinguished as follows: "behavioral transmission usually refers to the content of the information transmitted across generations (such as a specific skill or belief) and emotional programming refers to a process between the generations" [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory; the same passage appears in FSJ 4.2 (3) Kerr, M. Bowen Theory and Evo Theory 119-175].

The programming is described as "the basis for a behavioral inheritance system that seems as precise and predictable in the transmission of information across generations as the genetic inheritance system" [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory], and "information regulating individual development and behavior is encoded not only in the genes, but also in the relationship system" [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory].

**When and how it occurs**

"Emotional programming occurs through the ongoing interactions parents and other family members have with a child during his developmental years. Some programming may occur during intrauterine development" [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory]. "Differences in the intensity of family triangles result in siblings being programmed differently. In other words, parents typically focus more on one child than on the other(s). Chronic anxiety drives the more intense focus, not the reality needs of the child" [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory]. In the FSJ 9.2 formulation, "The more the parents focus on a child out of their own needs and fears, the more the child focuses automatically on the parents," and while "Myriad variations of content exist in this child focus," four elements are constant: "these four 'social cues' … parental attention, approval, expectations, and distress" [FSJ 9.2 Bowen Subjectivity, Homo Sapiens, and Science].

**Effects**

"It affects myriad automatic behaviors that govern much of people's lives, for better and for worse. For example, the programming can intensify or deintensify fear reactions to social stimuli" [Bowen Theory Secrets_Chapter09_Emotional_Programming]. "Emotional programming appears to affect a child's biochemical, physiological, psychological, and relationship functioning" [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory]. The more focused-on child "develops more of a 'relationship orientation' than his sibling(s)," whereas the less focused-on sibling "is more self-contained, has a more separate and distinct 'self'"; "the siblings develop different levels of differentiation" [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory].

**Relation to individuality and togetherness**

"Emotional programming occurs in the context of the interplay between the counterbalancing life forces of individuality and togetherness in parent-offspring relationships. Variations in the particular balance of these forces can explain variation in the intensity of the emotional programming that occurs, as well as variation in the capacity for self-regulation" [Bowen Theory Secrets_Chapter09_Emotional_Programming].

**Relation to emotion-concept theory (a proposal, not a settled claim)**

"What Bowen theory posits is that the individual learns how to function in the family via emotional processes and programming"; a child "learns (or is programmed) to think and feel what the family believes is appropriate for any given situation" [FSJ 14.2 Galloway How Emotions Are Made]. The author of that piece states this as a belief about what is compatible between the two theories ("This author believes…") rather than as an established finding [FSJ 14.2 Galloway How Emotions Are Made].

**On genes — a qualification, not a conflict**

Chapter 9 states the transmission occurs "by relationships and learning, not genes," while also noting that "the exploding field of epigenetics … opens the door to studying the interaction between emotional programming and gene expression. The research in this field is at too early a stage to form firm conclusions about such a process" [Bowen Theory Secrets_Chapter09_Emotional_Programming]. Separately, Kerr describes multigenerational emotional programming as having "a 'genetic-like' quality," meaning information is "encoded not only in the genes, but also in the relationship system" [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory]. These are not presented in the excerpts as competing definitions; the excerpts distinguish "genetic-like" transmission through relationships from genetic inheritance itself.

**Terminological note**

"Programming" is used in other bodies of knowledge "but not in the same way Bowen theory uses them," and "placing the term emotional in front of each term anchors them to the bridge—the emotional system—that Bowen theory constructs to link human behavior to evolution and the behavior of other species" [Bowen Theory Secrets_Chapter06_Emotional_Regression].
