## Definition and mechanism

- Emotional programming "occurs through the transmission of information across generations in a family by relationships and learning, not genes" [Bowen Theory Secrets_Chapter09_Emotional Programming].
- "The programming results from the verbal, nonverbal, and behavioral interactions that occur within and between the generations" [Bowen Theory Secrets_Chapter09_Emotional Programming].
- "Emotional programming occurs through the ongoing interactions parents and other family members have with a child during his developmental years. Some programming may occur during intrauterine development" [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory; repeated verbatim in FSJ 4.2 (3) Kerr, M. Bowen Theory and Evo Theory 119-175].
- "The most intense programming occurs in a person's parental triangle during development, but siblings and other family members have an important influence on it as well" [Bowen Theory Secrets_Chapter09_Emotional Programming].

## Relation to multigenerational transmission and "behavioral inheritance"

- "The multigenerational transmission process includes the emotional programming of one generation by the generations preceding it" [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory].
- "The programming is the basis for a behavioral inheritance system that seems as precise and predictable in the transmission of information across generations as the genetic inheritance system" [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory].
- Kerr distinguishes the two terms: "behavioral transmission usually refers to the content of the information transmitted across generations (such as a specific skill or belief) and emotional programming refers to a process between the generations" [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory].
- Kerr also describes this as "a 'genetic-like' quality" and states that "information regulating individual development and behavior is encoded not only in the genes, but also in the relationship system" [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory].
- On the gene question, the Chapter 9 source states programming occurs "not genes," while noting that epigenetics "opens the door to studying the interaction between emotional programming and gene expression," but that "The research in this field is at too early a stage to form firm conclusions about such a process" [Bowen Theory Secrets_Chapter09_Emotional Programming]. Kerr's use of "genetic-like" is presented in quotation marks as an analogy for precision and predictability of transmission, not as genetic transmission [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory].

## What it affects

- "Emotional programming appears to affect a child's biochemical, physiological, psychological, and relationship functioning" [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory].
- "People are not conscious of most of the emotional programming that occurs, but it affects myriad automatic behaviors that govern much of people's lives, for better and for worse. For example, the programming can intensify or deintensify fear reactions to social stimuli. Automatic responses have interconnected emotional, feeling, and subjective cognitive components" [Bowen Theory Secrets_Chapter09_Emotional Programming].
- "The process is anchored in the emotional system but includes complex feeling and psychological components" [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory].
- In one formulation, "Emotional programming refers to the specific effects that the nature of the parents' interactions with a child during development have on the child's ability to separate emotionally and develop a self" [FSJ 9.2 Bowen Subjectivity, Homo Sapiens, and Science]. This source adds: "The more the parents focus on a child out of their own needs and fears, the more the child focuses automatically on the parents. This reciprocal and ongoing process, which can be harmonious or contentious, heightens the child's reactivity to parental attention, approval, expectations, and distress." It names "four 'social cues'" — "parental attention, approval, expectations, and distress" [FSJ 9.2 Bowen Subjectivity, Homo Sapiens, and Science].

## Differential programming of siblings

- "Differences in the intensity of family triangles result in siblings being programmed differently. In other words, parents typically focus more on one child than on the other(s). Chronic anxiety drives the more intense focus, not the reality needs of the child" [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory].
- "Parents commonly say they are more involved with or worry more about one child because that child has more problems or needs but miss how they unwittingly program the child to need more of their energy and attention. Their unrealistic needs for and expectations of the child program him to have unrealistic needs for and expectations of them" [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory].
- "The more focused-on child develops more of a 'relationship orientation' than his sibling(s)... Growing up freer of the anxiety-driven components of family emotional process, the sibling(s) is more self-contained, has a more separate and distinct 'self'... In other words, the siblings develop different levels of differentiation" [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory].
- "If one child in a sibling group fulfills the family need, his or her siblings are relatively 'off the hook'" [FSJ 9.2 Bowen Subjectivity, Homo Sapiens, and Science].

## Context of individuality and togetherness

- "Emotional programming occurs in the context of the interplay between the counterbalancing life forces of individuality and togetherness in parent-offspring relationships. Variations in the particular balance of these forces can explain variation in the intensity of the emotional programming that occurs, as well as variation in the capacity for self-regulation" [Bowen Theory Secrets_Chapter09_Emotional Programming].
- "The child triggers emotionally programmed reactions in the parent simultaneously with the parent triggering emotional programming in the child" [Bowen Theory Secrets_Chapter09_Emotional Programming].
