- "Behavioral transmission and emotional programming both involve learning and relationships, but behavioral transmission usually refers to the content of the information transmitted across generations (such as a specific skill or belief) and emotional programming refers to a process between the generations." - "The multigenerational transmission process includes the emotional programming of one generation by the generations preceding it." [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory]
- "The programming is the basis for a behavioral inheritance system that seems as precise and predictable in the transmission of information across generations as the genetic inheritance system." [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory]
- "Multigenerational emotional programming has a 'genetic-like' quality because people predictably attach to others like themselves, to those with the same level of differentiation." [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory]

## What produces it

- "The programming results from the verbal, nonverbal, and behavioral interactions that occur within and between the generations. The most intense programming occurs in a person's parental triangle during development, but siblings and other family members have an important influence on it as well." [Bowen Theory Secrets_Chapter09_Emotional_Programming]
- "Some programming may occur during intrauterine development." [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory]
- "Differences in the intensity of family triangles result in siblings being programmed differently. In other words, parents typically focus more on one child than on the other(s). Chronic anxiety drives the more intense focus, not the reality needs of the child." [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory]
- "The more the parents focus on a child out of their own needs and fears, the more the child focuses automatically on the parents. This reciprocal and ongoing process, which can be harmonious or contentious, heightens the child's reactivity to parental attention, approval, expectations, and distress. Myriad variations of content exist in this child focus, but these four 'social cues' are consistent elements of the programming that occurs." [FSJ 9.2 Bowen Subjectivity, Homo Sapiens, and Science]
- "However, anxiety-driven parental over involvement with a child not only heightens his or her reactivity to the cues, but it also constrains the child's ability to formulate thoughts, opinions, and ideas on their own." [FSJ 9.2 Bowen Subjectivity, Homo Sapiens, and Science]

## What it affects

- "Emotional programming appears to affect a child's biochemical, physiological, psychological, and relationship functioning." [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory]
- "People are not conscious of most of the emotional programming that occurs, but it affects myriad automatic behaviors that govern much of people's lives, for better and for worse. For example, the programming can intensify or deintensify fear reactions to social stimuli. Automatic responses have interconnected emotional, feeling, and subjective cognitive components." [Bowen Theory Secrets_Chapter09_Emotional_Programming]
- "The more focused-on child develops more of a 'relationship orientation' than his sibling(s)... In other words, the siblings develop different levels of differentiation." [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory]
