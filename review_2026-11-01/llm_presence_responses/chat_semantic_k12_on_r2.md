**Definition(s) given in the sources**

- "Emotional programming refers to the specific effects that the nature of the parents' interactions with a child during development have on the child's ability to separate emotionally and develop a self." [FSJ 9.2 Bowen Subjectivity, Homo Sapiens, and Science]
- "Emotional programming occurs through the ongoing interactions parents and other family members have with a child during his developmental years. - "The programming results from the verbal, nonverbal, and behavioral interactions that occur within and between the generations. The most intense programming occurs in a person's parental triangle during development, but siblings and other family members have an important influence on it as well. People are not conscious of most of the emotional programming that occurs, but it affects myriad automatic behaviors that govern much of people's lives, for better and for worse." [Bowen Theory Secrets_Chapter09_Emotional_Programming]

**Relation to behavioral transmission**

"Behavioral transmission and emotional programming both involve learning and relationships, but behavioral transmission usually refers to the content of the information transmitted across generations (such as a specific skill or belief) and emotional programming refers to a process between the generations." [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory]

**Relation to the multigenerational transmission process**

"Bowen theory's concept of a multigenerational transmission process is broader than what biologists generally mean by behavioral transmission. The multigenerational transmission process includes the emotional programming of one generation by the generations preceding it. The programming is the basis for a behavioral inheritance system that seems as precise and predictable in the transmission of information across generations as the genetic inheritance system." [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory]

"Multigenerational emotional programming has a 'genetic-like' quality because people predictably attach to others like themselves, to those with the same level of differentiation." [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory]

**Mechanism / differential programming of siblings**

"Differences in the intensity of family triangles result in siblings being programmed differently. In other words, parents typically focus more on one child than on the other(s). Chronic anxiety drives the more intense focus, not the reality needs of the child. ... The process is anchored in the emotional system but includes complex feeling and psychological components." [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory]

"The more the parents focus on a child out of their own needs and fears, the more the child focuses automatically on the parents. This reciprocal and ongoing process, which can be harmonious or contentious, heightens the child's reactivity to parental attention, approval, expectations, and distress. Myriad variations of content exist in this child focus, but these four 'social cues' are consistent elements of the programming that occurs." [FSJ 9.2 Bowen Subjectivity, Homo Sapiens, and Science]
