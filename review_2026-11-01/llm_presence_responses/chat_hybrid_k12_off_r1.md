## Definitions given in the sources

The sources give several formulations rather than a single definition:

- "The phenomenon of emotional programming occurs through the transmission of information across generations in a family by relationships and learning, not genes" [Bowen Theory Secrets_Chapter09_Emotional_Programming].
- "The multigenerational transmission process includes the emotional programming of one generation by the generations preceding it" [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory]; the same text appears in the duplicate copy [FSJ 4.2 (3) Kerr, M. Bowen Theory and Evo Theory 119-175].
- "Emotional programming refers to the specific effects that the nature of the parents' interactions with a child during development have on the child's ability to separate emotionally and develop a self" [FSJ 9.2 Bowen Subjectivity, Homo Sapiens, and Science].
- "What Bowen theory posits is that the individual learns how to function in the family via emotional processes and programming" [FSJ 14.2 Galloway How Emotions Are Made].

**Note on variation between sources:** the sources place different emphases — emotional programming as a cross-generational transmission process [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory], versus as the specific developmental effects of parents' interactions on a child's ability to separate and develop a self [FSJ 9.2 Bowen Subjectivity, Homo Sapiens, and Science]. The sources do not present these as competing definitions and do not resolve the relationship between them.

## How it is described as occurring

- "The programming results from the verbal, nonverbal, and behavioral interactions that occur within and between the generations" [Bowen Theory Secrets_Chapter09_Emotional_Programming].
- "Emotional programming occurs through the ongoing interactions parents and other family members have with a child during his developmental years. Some programming may occur during intrauterine development" [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory].
- "The most intense programming occurs in a person's parental triangle during development, but siblings and other family members have an important influence on it as well" [Bowen Theory Secrets_Chapter09_Emotional_Programming].
- "The more the parents focus on a child out of their own needs and fears, the more the child focuses automatically on the parents. This reciprocal and ongoing process, which can be harmonious or contentious, heightens the child's reactivity to parental attention, approval, expectations, and distress" [FSJ 9.2 Bowen Subjectivity, Homo Sapiens, and Science]. These four "social cues" are described as "consistent elements of the programming that occurs" [FSJ 9.2 Bowen Subjectivity, Homo Sapiens, and Science].
- "The child triggers emotionally programmed reactions in the parent simultaneously with the parent triggering emotional programming in the child" [Bowen Theory Secrets_Chapter09_Emotional_Programming].

## Distinction from behavioral transmission

"Behavioral transmission and emotional programming both involve learning and relationships, but behavioral transmission usually refers to the content of the information transmitted across generations (such as a specific skill or belief) and emotional programming refers to a process between the generations" [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory].

## What it is said to affect

- "Emotional programming appears to affect a child's biochemical, physiological, psychological, and relationship functioning" [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory].
- "People are not conscious of most of the emotional programming that occurs, but it affects myriad automatic behaviors that govern much of people's lives, for better and for worse. For example, the programming can intensify or deintensify fear reactions to social stimuli. Automatic responses have interconnected emotional, feeling, and subjective cognitive components" [Bowen Theory Secrets_Chapter09_Emotional_Programming].
- "anxiety-driven parental over involvement with a child not only heightens his or her reactivity to the cues, but it also constrains the child's ability to formulate thoughts, opinions, and ideas on their own" [FSJ 9.2 Bowen Subjectivity, Homo Sapiens, and Science].

## Relation to individuality–togetherness

"Emotional programming occurs in the context of the interplay between the counterbalancing life forces of individuality and togetherness in parent-offspring relationships. Variations in the particular balance of these forces can explain variation in the intensity of the emotional programming that occurs, as well as variation in the capacity for self-regulation" [Bowen Theory Secrets_Chapter09_Emotional_Programming].

## Differential programming of siblings

- "Differences in the intensity of family triangles result in siblings being programmed differently. In other words, parents typically focus more on one child than on the other(s). Chronic anxiety drives the more intense focus, not the reality needs of the child" [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory].
- "The more focused-on child develops more of a 'relationship orientation' than his sibling(s)... Growing up freer of the anxiety-driven components of family emotional process, the sibling(s) is more self-contained, has a more separate and distinct 'self,' and is more consistent in defining and pursuing life goals. In other words, the siblings develop different levels of differentiation" [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory].
- "If one child in a sibling group fulfills the family need, his or her siblings are relatively 'off the hook.'... The less involved child is less likely to misperceive social interactions, less likely to overreact to them, and better able to self-regulate" [FSJ 9.2 Bowen Subjectivity, Homo Sapiens, and Science].

## "Genetic-like" quality

- "The programming is the basis for a behavioral inheritance system that seems as precise and predictable in the transmission of information across generations as the genetic inheritance system" [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory].
- "Multigenerational emotional programming has a 'genetic-like' quality because people predictably attach to others like themselves, to those with the same level of differentiation" [FSJ 4.2 Kerr Bowen Theory and Evolutionary Theory].
- "information regulating individual development and behavior is encoded not only in the genes, but also in the relationship system" [FSJ 4.2 (3) Kerr, M. Bowen Theory and Evo Theory 119-175].

## On genes and epigenetics

"Important to note, however, is that the exploding field of epigenetics, which examines the interplay between the environment and the switching off and on of genes, opens the door to studying the interaction between emotional programming and gene expression. The research in this field is at too early a stage to form firm conclusions about such a process" [Bowen Theory Secrets_Chapter09_Emotional_Programming].

## Related framing in the Barrett discussion

"what the family believes is appropriate for any given situation" learning is described as the child being "programmed" to "think and feel" — "The child, by learning what words, concepts, and emotions mean, in their family, learns (or is programmed) to think and feel what the family believes is appropriate for any given situation" [FSJ 14.2 Galloway How Emotions Are Made]. This passage attributes the underlying account to Barrett's theory and notes that "Barrett makes no specific reference to how one functions in a family, while the concept of how one functions, versus how one is, is important in Bowen theory" [FSJ 14.2 Galloway How Emotions Are Made].
